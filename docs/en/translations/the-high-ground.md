## Introduction

This memo attempts to answer one question: from 2026 to 2031, in the field of software and computing outside of foundation model providers, where will the “high ground” emerge?

The backdrop is the rapid spread of AI: on one hand it keeps entering a wide range of consumer and enterprise software scenarios, and on the other it is increasingly used to write software.

## AI in consumer and enterprise software

Predicting that AI will keep permeating software does not require taking on much risk. Compared with the current state, it will bring about four major changes:

1. **Generalist systems will keep growing at the expense of shrinking, highly specialized systems.** This comes from the general understanding capability shared by all major LLMs.
2. **Software systems can be controlled and customized through natural language.**
3. **Software can acquire self-awareness.** Feeding code and user behavior into an LLM will produce capabilities that are nearly emergent, forming software capable of understanding itself.
4. **Context occupies the primary position in highly generalist systems.** Specialization no longer comes from a predetermined software architecture, but from the broad acquisition and careful use of the user's and business's most relevant context at runtime or at inference time.

## AI in software creation

Just as clearly, AI is significantly changing how software is written. We can also describe this change along four dimensions:

1. **The cost of creating software will fall.** There will be more software in the world.
2. **The people who write software will change.** The barrier to entry is getting lower. People without general technical skills but with specialized knowledge and judgment can also direct general-purpose models.
3. **Software written by LLMs will become the median, that is, mediocre software.** Leading companies that can truly win in a market niche will be led by software architects with a distinctive view of efficient and scalable systems engineering, or built by craftspeople who combine market insight and unique taste with the general capabilities of LLMs.
4. **AI is raising the level of participation.** This shows both in who can write software, and in how end users change software to suit their preferences. Therefore, the best systems must be open and adaptable, allowing a broader developer community to contribute, while giving end users a high degree of customization.

## A brief note on social media

Even before machines entered social media on a massive scale, people's satisfaction with social media was clearly low. The platforms were fragmented, while ownership was increasingly concentrated in a few hands. Algorithms push users toward content that spins in an endless cycle of stimulation and desire.

When you hear conversations in restaurants and bars, it is hardly an exaggeration to say that people often talk about how they use social media with disgust and self-blame. We are reluctant to let children touch it, and social media does not enrich our lives or relationships either.

Of course, there are still a few communities that gain more benefit than harm from social media: those who gather around a skill, a shared passion, or a shared interest. But these truly valuable corners are mostly just oases in a desert.

Meaningful digital interaction between people still mostly takes place in private spaces, such as instant messaging, chats, a small number of forums, and email correspondence.

## The high ground

The high ground will emerge at an intersection like this: humans interacting with one another, while also interacting with a system that is highly capable, customizable, personal, pleasurable, simple to use, and able to maintain users' trust. Here is a breakdown of those traits one by one.

### Interaction

As quantum physicists know, the universe emerges more from how things interact than from what things are in themselves. As machines slowly acquire the ability to understand human natural language, we will want more to let information form and flow through interaction, rather than building and maintaining static information structures.

### Capability

This means having the most advanced Agent capabilities, while also staying accurate and avoiding major errors.

### Customizable

This means end users—individuals, small businesses, or enterprises—can change the system to the maximum extent according to their preferences, and can do so easily and intuitively.

### Personal

This trait goes beyond customization, though some personalization does grow out of customization. It means a system gradually makes the end user feel that the system is **their own**. It has already adapted to the user's unique life background and personality.

### Pleasurable

The system itself surprises and delights the end user. In interacting with it, people can feel wonder and relief.

### Simple

Starting to use the software and continuing to interact with it are both uncomplicated.

### Winning and keeping trust

The product and the company behind it must present themselves as trustworthy; use the user's context transparently; not abuse the user's trust, and not expose users to unacceptable security risks.

By realizing the traits above and then occupying the high ground, a system can open a crucial positive cycle between “use” and “context”:

> The more often it is used, the deeper the context that accumulates; the deeper the context, the more accurate the system's behavior; the more accurately the system behaves—especially compared with similar products or competitors—the greater the user's desire to keep using it.

We believe that as long as a system can maintain the qualities above, it should be owned by the user, as much as possible.

## Closing thoughts

Finally, let us understand this in another way.

Imagine a garden. Place yourself inside it in your mind. You choose what to plant, and you tend it carefully. The garden may be fenced, setting it apart from other people's gardens and spaces. It is a safe place, yet not entirely separate from the world. Weather, climate, and the outside world still impose constraints on you.

Over time, the garden will show how you responded to those constraints, and reflect the decisions you made over the years. Eventually, the garden will mirror who you are.

Inside your garden lives an entity. It is rooted in that garden, yet it is intelligent, understands the world, and is extraordinarily capable. It is mainly there to guide and help you, and it can also interact with others. It understands deeply every inch of soil, every flower beginning to bloom, and every fruit in that garden, while being able to connect everything in it with the vast outside world.

Like the garden, this entity will also gradually mirror you. You can send it outside the garden; even so, it still carries the color given by the choices you made on this soil, and remains rooted here.

Earendil is building this entity, while also building this garden. Our system emerges from both.

We call it Lefos.
